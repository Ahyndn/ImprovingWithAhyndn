# Chapter 10: Questioning My Own Hustle

## After My Horrible Career Events
As another year was about to begin, I continued to barely cope with my new working methods required to complete my job because of my unexpected event. I was full-time still and had asked if part-time would an option; that was not allowed by my store manager. Understandably, I was not happy remaining at my job.

At work, some staff starting circulating the question about my lack of career growth given my skill set. This was not unusual because the HR generalist was already not happy about my career earlier this year and had already discussed careers, including options at corporate, with me. Additionally, a former general manager mentor from earlier spoke to me to highly consider careers at the company by discussing with the store manager. My non-existent growth by the store manager was becoming very evident to date.

A store manager termination was even proposed as an option by the HR generalist. Any inappropriate management of talent was implied as the concern [[71](https://www.ontario.ca/document/employment-standard-act-policy-and-interpretation-manual), [71.i](https://www.ontario.ca/document/your-guide-employment-standards-act-0/reprisals)-[71.iii](https://www.ontario.ca/page/reprisals-against-workers-employers), [72](https://www.ohrc.on.ca/en), 72.i-[72.ii](https://www.ohrc.on.ca/en/policy-ableism-and-discrimination-based-disability/7-reprisal), [73](https://www.ontario.ca/document/guide-occupational-health-and-safety-act), [73.i](https://www.ontario.ca/document/guide-occupational-health-and-safety-act/part-vi-reprisals-employer-prohibited)]. **Ultimately, I declined the HR generalist because I respected my store manager and I politely stated that I would like to develop software someday.** My career choice was heavily respected by HR generalist.
